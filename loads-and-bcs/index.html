    Applying Loads and Boundary Conditions

    My load arrows aren't changing when I update the magnitude — is something wrong?

    No — this is expected behavior in Abaqus/CAE. Load arrow sizes are uniform and unrelated to the magnitude of the prescribed condition. They are visual indicators of load direction and location only, not scaled representations of magnitude. The only exception is when an analytical field is used to define a spatially varying distribution.

    How do I apply a line load?

    A true line load (force per unit length) cannot be applied directly to an interior edge in Abaqus — it can only be applied to free surface edges. For interior locations, convert it to an equivalent pressure over a narrow strip:

    Steps:

    1. Choose a strip width w and partition your geometry to create that strip
    2. Calculate the equivalent pressure: p = q / w
    3. where q is the line load (force/length) and w is the strip width (length)
    4. Example: a line load of 12 lbf/in over a 2 in strip = 6 lbf/in²
    5. Apply this pressure to the partitioned strip face in the Load Module

    How do I verify the pressure I applied?

    1. In the Step Module, edit your Field Output request
    2. Under Loads, add P (pressure) to the selected outputs, then click OK
    3. Run the job and open results in the Visualization Module
    4. Select PLOAD as the field variable to confirm the applied pressure distribution

    How do I apply a distributed moment?

    1. In the Load Module, go to Tools > Analytical Field > Create — name it and select Expression field
    2. Enter the expression describing your moment distribution
    3. In the Mesh Module, create a node set: Tools > Set > Create, select Node, then select the nodes where the moment acts
    4. Back in the Load Module, click Create Load > Mechanical > Moment
    5. Click Sets to select your node set, then choose your analytical field under Distribution
    6. Fill in the moment direction (CM1, CM2, or CM3) and click OK

    !!! note The moment is applied per node and has units of force × length. To verify, request CF (Concentrated forces and moments) in Field Output and visualize CM after the job runs.

    How do I define contact between parts?

    Contact is defined in the Interactions Module and requires two steps.

    Step 1 — Define the contact property:

    1. Go to the Interactions Module
    2. Click Interaction > Property > Create and select Contact
    3. Click Mechanical and add Tangential Behavior
    4. Choose the Lagrange friction formulation and enter your coefficient of friction

    Step 2 — Define the surfaces:

    1. Click Interaction > Create and choose Surface-to-Surface Contact
    2. Define the Master Surface and the Slave Surface
    3. Master surface nodes can penetrate the slave surface; slave nodes cannot penetrate the master
    4. Choose the stiffer or coarser-meshed surface as the master
    5. Select the sliding formulation:
    6. Finite sliding — allows arbitrary separation, sliding, and rotation (most general)
    7. Small sliding — limits contact to a small portion of the master surface; requires NLGEOM to be enabled

    For most 3D structural models, small sliding is the standard choice. Refer to the Abaqus User's Manual for more detail on which formulation suits your problem.
